# Publications and Telegram Posting

## Overview

This document provides a comprehensive description of the **publications** feature of the TRVL project, covering:

- How publications are stored and managed (drafts, scheduled posts, published posts, soft-deleted posts).
- The workflow for creating, editing, deleting and publishing content.
- Integration with the **Telegram** channel (TRVL) - updating channel description, sending text or photo posts, editing and deleting messages. A pin command is available for test posts; scheduled publication pinning is not part of the publications UI.
- Console commands and cron schedules that automate publishing tasks.
- Configuration requirements.
- Detailed database schema and table relationships.
- Error handling and validation rules.

Domain rules and persistence adapters are implemented in the **Shared layer** (see the root `README.md` for the architecture and setup). The web UI, controllers, configuration and console entry points are in the Application layer. Shared classes are under `app/shared/Publications`, `app/shared/Settings` and `app/shared/Telegram`.

## Architecture

| Layer | Responsibility |
|------|-----------------|
| **Framework / infrastructure** | Yii and Composer dependencies; the project has no separate Core layer. |
| **Shared** | Business functionality - publications, forum integration, Telegram channel integration. |
| **Application** | UI controllers, console commands, configuration, orchestration of use-cases. |

The **Publications** module lives entirely in the **Shared** layer and does **not** depend on any Application code.

## Domain Model

### Database Schema

The publications feature uses four tables to manage the lifecycle of content, plus one table to hold its tunables:

| Table | Purpose | Status Indicators |
|-------|---------|-------------------|
| `publications_post` | Main publications table | - `telegram_id IS NULL` = Scheduled (future `published_at`)<br>- `telegram_id IS NOT NULL` = Published<br>- `published_at` in past = Due for publishing<br>- `part_released = FALSE` = A part waiting for the part above it |
| `publications_draft` | Draft publications | - Ready for editing/scheduling<br>- `part_group_id`, `part_order`, `part_released` since `m261017_000001_keep_part_state_outside_posts`: a part sent to drafts keeps its group and the wait it held there |
| `publications_edited` | Edit history of published posts | - `edited_at IS NULL` = Needs update in Telegram<br>- `edited_at IS NOT NULL` = Already updated in Telegram |
| `publications_deleted` | Soft-delete archive | - `deleted_at IS NULL` = Pending deletion from Telegram<br>- `deleted_at IS NOT NULL` = The channel no longer holds the message (deleted there, or already gone when `deleteDue()` asked for it)<br>- the same three part columns: a part that rests here keeps its group and its wait, so a part carried back into the posts table comes into the queue the way the parts above it leave it<br>- one exception: the debt a saving button leaves when a record that already reached the channel goes to drafts (`insertChannelDeletionTicket()`) is written with its `telegram_id` and **no group columns** — a second row claiming the same `part_order` would be read back by `findGroupRows()` as one more part of the publication |
| `publications_setting` | Tunables of the publications page | - one row per code (`code` → `value`, `updated_at`)<br>- seeded from `PublicationSettingsService::defaults()` by `m260925_000001_create_publications_setting` |

A publication whose text goes past the 4096-character limit of one Telegram message is stored as several `publications_post` (or `publications_draft`) rows, one per part: the rows are written by a single `createPosts()`/`createDrafts()` call inside a transaction, `published_at` puts each part the configured gap after the previous one (`partsOffsetMinutes`, one minute by default, never shorter than the `publish-due` schedule), and the parts are ordinary records, ordered by `id`. The form keeps that shift out of its own arithmetic: the preview shows it instead, each part's card carrying in the corner of its bubble the field of the slot moved on by one `partsOffsetMinutes` for every part field that stands before it — the same expression `saveParts()` writes into `published_at`, so what the card promises is what the row stores. See «Время части в углу предпросмотра» in [README.md](README.md). Each part has its own `title`; the channel adds that heading (including the «Часть N» marker when numbering is enabled) to the message at send time, while the stored `text` remains the editor's text. Each row keeps its own `image_urls`, so a part is sent to the channel with the photos it holds: the form gives every part a field of its own (`publicationPartImages[]`, the album of the first part being the shared `publicationImages` block, which since 2026-10-22 stands in the card of that part while the text is cut and under the list of parts while it is one piece), and what a field holds on submit is what that row gets — an album that belongs to no part of its own stays with the first one. Beside the caret split the form offers three manual modes for the text as a whole — by paragraphs, by lines, by sentences (`splitWholeText`, the buttons carrying `data-split-whole`): one part per boundary of the chosen kind, however short it comes out, with a piece too long for one message still cut down by `splitIntoParts`. All four handles of that row — the three modes and «Разделить» beside them — wear `btn-outline-success`, the green this form uses to say a control belongs to the parts. Two buttons under each part's album field move the whole list to the neighbouring part (`moveImageGroup`, `data-move-album`): the received pictures come in behind the ones the receiving album already held — `unionPictures`, so a link both fields showed is named once — and the field they left stands empty, while the last part has no «next» button and moving an empty list is refused so a stray click cannot empty the neighbour's field. The order the receiving album comes out with is written into its own mask field, so a link and a file that were interleaved before the move stay interleaved after it. See «Строки альбома и их порядок» in [README.md](README.md). See «Разбивка длинного текста на части» in [README.md](README.md). Every album field also carries a file picker of its own — «Файлы публикации» / «Файлы этой части» (`publicationImageFiles[]` for the shared field, `publicationPartImageFiles{N-1}[]` for part *N*, the first block's picker hidden and disabled exactly like its links field): a file chosen there is stored under the web root and becomes one more link of that same album, so `image_urls` stays a list of links and neither the record nor the channel has to know anything about the upload. See «Файлы альбома» in [README.md](README.md).

A publication cut into parts can also be published **one part at a time**, which is the switch «Работать с частями по отдельности» of the form (it stood as «Публиковать части отдельно» until 2026-10-21, when the row of handles of a part came under it besides its queue — the name moved, the identifiers did not) (`SiteController::partsPublication()`, kept in the session under `partsPublication` exactly as the album rows and the links switches are). Its rows carry three more columns of `publications_post` (`m261010_000001_add_publication_part_sequence`, which also puts an index over `part_group_id, part_order` there — on the two databases of this machine that index keeps the name it got from an earlier, unrecorded migration, `idx_publications_post_part_order`, see «Testing» below): `part_group_id` — 32 hex characters naming one group, `part_order` — the place of the row in it, counting from 1, and `part_released` — whether the row stands in the queue. `createStagedGroup()` writes all the parts of the group in one transaction with `part_released` true for the first row and false for every row below it, so a half-written group cannot happen and the queue never sees a part out of its turn. The queue is where that column is read: `findDueForPublishing()` takes `telegram_id IS NULL AND part_released = TRUE AND published_at <= now`, so an unreleased part is not a scheduled post that happens to have come early — it is invisible to the task. Two of the other three tables carry the queue state as well since `m261017_000001_keep_part_state_outside_posts`, which adds `part_group_id`, `part_order` and `part_released` to `publications_draft` and to `publications_deleted`, the two group columns to `publications_edited`, and an index over `part_group_id, part_order` to the first two — the reason being that a part of a group moves between the blocks of the page one row at a time and the state of its queue has to travel with it: a part that was held back and sent to drafts comes back held back instead of slipping into the queue ahead of the part that holds it up. The two columns those indexes stand on come from the same file: it lays `part_group_id` and `part_order` into all three tables before it indexes the pair, so a fresh database reaches the `createIndex` with the columns already under it — proved on an empty database 2026-10-19, where all 23 migration files applied and the four publications tables came out carrying the group (see «Testing» below). On this machine those two columns stood in all four tables before the file was written at all, laid there by `m261009_000001_add_publication_part_order` — a row of both migration histories with no migration file (see «Testing» below). What `publications_edited` never takes is the released flag, and `archiveEdited()` writes it neither, though it now writes the group id and the place: the archive is the history of one channel message, which no list reads as part of a queue. `insertParts()` still writes the three columns only when a group id was handed to it, while `hydrateOne()`/`hydrateAll()` read them as «an ordinary row» (`partGroupId` null, `partReleased` true) when a query did not ask for them.

Whether a waiting part may be started is a fourth answer, computed in the same `SELECT` (`partColumns()`) rather than stored: `part_releasable` is true when the row is unreleased and the row of the same group with `part_order - 1` carries a `telegram_id` — the part above has really reached the channel, not merely left the queue. `PublicationsService::releasePart()` is the only way through that door: it reads the group, refuses a part that is not there, one already released, and one whose neighbour above has not been published, normalises and checks the time the button was given against the time of the part above, and hands the group, the order and the time to a conditional `UPDATE ... WHERE part_released = FALSE`, which refuses the second click of a race with «Часть уже поставлена в очередь.». The ordinary «Опубликовать» of a row cannot step over the sequence either: `publishPostNow()` asks `assertPartInOrder()` first, which refuses a part whose group holds an earlier part with no `telegram_id`. What the sequence does not do is hold a row still inside its own block: `deletePost()` and `deleteDraft()` move the single row they are given into `publications_deleted`, `movePostToDraft()` moves it into drafts — and when that row had already reached the channel, the move leaves a debt there: `insertChannelDeletionTicket()` writes the `publications_deleted` row whose `deleted_at` the periodic task will stamp, since a draft carries no `telegram_id` for `deleteDue()` to work from — and the list offers every one of those buttons to every part — the door the sequence keeps shut is the queue alone. The two refusals that once answered a part of a group («Часть отдельной публикации нельзя редактировать/удалить отдельно.») left the code with that reading.

A group is no longer one block: the parts of one publication lie in the three lists at the same time, and the row that moves keeps belonging to the group it was written into. `findGroupRows()` is the reading that spans the tables — a `UNION ALL` over `publications_post`, `publications_draft` and `publications_deleted`, each row naming its `habitat` (`channel` for a posts row that carries a `telegram_id`, `post`, `draft` or `deleted` for the rest) and each read carrying `partGroupTotal` — three `COUNT(*)`s, one over each of the three tables, added together by `partGroupTotal()` — so the number a part wears is how many parts the publication has across the three lists, not how many of them still stand in the queue. `PublicationsUi::partBadge()` draws that count as `<span class="badge bg-primary-subtle text-primary mt-2">Часть N из M</span>` — the same badge family the status of a row belongs to — and stays silent for a row with no group or no place of its own, for a total it was not given, and for a total of one — the last living part of a group is a publication on its own. `findPartGroup()` keeps its old posts-only reading: the queue is the only thing that still asks for the parts of a group, and `part_releasable` is computed against `publications_post` alone.

The form follows the group, not the row that was clicked: `SiteController::actionPublicationPartGroup()` answers `PublicationsService::formRowsOfGroup()` with every live part of it — the parts that rest in the deleted block stay there, except the one row the reader opened from that block, which comes along with them. Each block of the form then wears a mark over its own slot field that names where that part came from and stands: `writeSlot()` puts one pill of its own glyph and colour — `badge rounded-pill fw-semibold px-3 py-1` — with the glyph inside it at the left of the word (`bi …` behind a `me-1`), so the mark is a single box and the glyph reads the pill's own `line-height: 1` instead of standing beside it in an `icon-box sm` and setting the height of the row. `HABITAT_BADGES` holds six states — «Форум» (`bi-collection`, info), «В канале» (`bi-telegram`, success), «Публикации» (`bi-journal-text`, primary), «Черновики» (`bi-file-earmark-text`, warning), «Удалённые» (`bi-trash`, danger) and «Без блока» (`bi-pencil`, secondary), the last one worn by a part the reader wrote and by one a cut gave birth to, so the row of the label is never left bare. The slot itself (`publicationPartSlot[]`) travels to the server as the JSON of that row — its id, the block the action moves it back to and the habitat the mark shows — while `SiteController::publicationSlotsFromRequest()` reads only the id and the block out of it and leaves the habitat to the page. A fill of the forum brings a slot of its own (`forumSlot()`, `{id: null, source: 'forum', habitat: 'forum'}`) into `loadText()` and `setTextParts()`, so a part taken from a topic or a thread says so and still writes a new row: with an empty id `saveGroupFromForm()` never reaches the `source:id` key it looks the home row up by. `saveGroupFromForm()` reads three actions off the form, and each of them states its own rule about blocks. «Опубликовать» gathers the whole group into the publications block: it gives a planned part the time of its own queue row, carries a drafted or a restored part into the posts table, and sends a part that reached the channel to the channel edit, the archive and the row both keeping the text the form brought. «Сохранить» carries the whole group into the drafts block: a drafted part keeps its row and changes only its content, a part standing in the publications block leaves that table for a draft, and a part the channel already holds writes a deletion ticket beside itself, while a part resting in the deleted block comes back as a draft with the deletion it was waiting for cancelled. «Сохранить по местам» (`action=home`, the button the form shows only while it carries a group) is the rule that used to belong to «Сохранить», kept as an action of its own: it writes every part back into the very row its slot names, leaving the block, the `telegram_id` and the time of that row alone, and asks the channel to edit the message of a part that has already gone out. A part the form invented goes to the block the action names. A part the form dropped goes to the deleted block. The queue rows of a loaded group are open even with the switch off, and the order they must keep is asked of the action that reads them: `partTimesEnforced = button.value === 'publish'`, so a group whose stored times do not ascend goes through both «Сохранить» and «Сохранить по местам», while «Опубликовать» is refused in the field itself (`setCustomValidity()` with the words the row has always used — «Время части должно быть позже времени предыдущей части.»). A group that is already stored is not re-checked on the server: `saveGroupFromForm()` takes each queue row as it is given. A part carried into the posts table from drafts or from the deleted list takes the queue flag of the parts standing above it, not its own: `standsAt($place, $released)` is given the running chain the loop keeps, so a drafted part that comes back behind a part which has not gone out enters the block waiting as well, and only a part with every neighbour above it open to the queue goes in open.

Every one of the four tables also carries a `formatting jsonb NOT NULL DEFAULT '[]'` column (`m260926_000001_add_formatting_to_publications`) — the highlighting of that row's `text`, kept as a list of Telegram message entities: `['type' => …, 'offset' => …, 'length' => …]`, plus `url` for a `text_link`, with offsets and lengths counted in UTF-16 code units, which is what the Bot API asks for. The text itself stays plain — no markup characters in it — and `parse_mode` is never sent, so the list is always read against the text it decorates, and an empty list (the column default, and therefore every row written before the editor existed) simply means plain text. A long publication is stored one row per part, so each row holds the entities of its own text. See «Форматирование текста» in [README.md](README.md).

Every one of the four tables carries a `button jsonb NOT NULL DEFAULT '{}'` column beside it (`m260927_000001_add_button_to_publications`) — the keyboard of that row as a list of pairs `[['text' => …, 'url' => …], …]`, one button per row Telegram draws under the message. The column needed no migration to hold a list: `LinkButtons::fromStored()` reads the list a new row writes, the single object a row written before the list holds, the empty object and `[]` of a message without buttons and the `null` of a row written before the column existed, so the default `{}` still means «no keyboard». A part of a long publication gets the buttons of its own row exactly as it gets the album and the entities of its own row. See «Кнопки-ссылки» in [README.md](README.md).

Every one of the four tables carries a `text` column `title` beside it (`m260928_000001_add_title_to_publications`, `NOT NULL DEFAULT ''`) — the heading of that row: the bold first line the channel puts over its text. It is stored apart from `text` and never inside it, so the message the channel gets is composed at send time (`PublicationData::messageText()` and `messageEntities()`), and an empty string — what every row written before the column holds — is a message that starts with its text. A part of a long publication gets the heading of its own row exactly as it gets the album, the entities and the button of its own row; when it has one, the «Часть N» line moves out of the text and to the end of that heading, so the part opens with «Заголовок. Часть 2» while the row keeps the text it was typed with — unless the heading already opens with that very number, which is what the form writes into the field when «Нумерация частей» and «Первая строка — в заголовок» stand on together, and then the number line simply leaves the text and the heading stays as it was typed. See «Заголовок части» in [README.md](README.md).

Every one of the four tables carries a `title_formatting jsonb NOT NULL DEFAULT '[]'` beside that heading (`m261024_000001_add_title_formatting_to_publications`, 36 lines and 1 052 bytes, with a symmetric `safeDown()`) — the highlighting of the heading itself, in the same entity-list shape as `formatting`, with offsets and lengths counted from the beginning of the heading rather than of the message. The repository carries it in its own `CONTENT_COLUMNS` (`'id, text, image_urls, formatting, button, title, title_formatting, '`) for every one of its four writes and for hydration, and `PublicationData` took a `titleFormatting` parameter at the tail of its constructor, so no existing call of it had to be retyped. The whole-line rule that used to stand in `MessageEntities::underHeading()` is taken out of the composition: the method now answers `$entities = $own->toArray()` where the ternary branch used to answer `$own->isEmpty() ? [['type' => 'bold', 'offset' => 0, 'length' => $units]] : $own->toArray()`. A heading that carries no spans of its own therefore goes out with no spans at all, and the body is still shifted behind the heading line by the same formula as before (the length of the heading, the empty line after it, minus the number the heading took out of the text). Nothing in the column says «marked / not marked» and nothing ever did — an empty list simply means an unmarked heading now, which is why the column still needs no second one. What the rule used to do the form does instead: four checkboxes of a heading block lay their own marks over a heading that has none, on the way out of the page rather than on the way in. Rows written while the composition did the stamping carry no marks of their own, so taking the rule out alone would have quieted them into plain text, and `m261025_000001_stamp_bold_on_headings_without_marks` (66 lines, 2 352 bytes) exists for them: it walks the four tables under one condition, `title <> '' AND title_formatting = '[]'::jsonb` (`WITHOUT_MARKS`), and updates each row it finds with `boldLine()` — one `bold` from offset 0 to `MessageEntities::utf16Length($title)`, the same measure every span the channel gets is counted by, so the fill cannot drift from the code that reads it back. Its `safeDown()` is empty on purpose: the reverse step has no value to take back, and a row cannot say which of its marks came from the rule and which the reader set himself, so an honest down() would delete the reader's own highlighting along with the stamped one.

Publications created from forum elements are additionally tracked in two link tables, one row per forum element:

| Table | Purpose | Status Indicators |
|-------|---------|-------------------|
| `publications_topic_map` | Links `topic.id` to a Telegram message | - no row = topic not seen<br>- `telegram_id IS NULL` = marked viewed («Просмотрено»)<br>- `telegram_id` set = published to the channel |
| `publications_post_map` | Links `post.id` to a Telegram message | - no row = post not seen<br>- `telegram_id IS NULL` = marked viewed («Просмотрено»)<br>- `telegram_id` set = published to the channel |

Both tables are written only through `ForumPublicationMapGatewayInterface` (see below). A topic that has exactly one post and that post count as a single element: marking or publishing either one writes the same state into the map row of the other, and a `telegram_id` the twin already has is never overwritten. Topics with several posts are not mirrored — one processed post says nothing about the rest.

The forum block filters («С изображениями», «С привязанными постами», «С ссылками», «Кол-во изображений», «Кол-во ссылок») and their session/URL sync are documented in [README.md](README.md), section «Фильтры блока «Форум»».

### Data Contracts and Interfaces

- **`PublicationData` (DTO)** - Represents a publication record with fields:
  - `id` (int): Primary key
  - `title` (string): The heading of the record — the first line the channel puts over its text, wearing the marks of `titleFormatting` and nothing else; empty when the message starts with its text
  - `text` (string): Publication content (1-4096 characters, one record per part of a long publication)
  - `imageUrls` (string[]): Array of image URLs
  - `formatting` (MessageEntities): The inline highlighting of `text` — the entity list of that row; empty for plain text
  - `titleFormatting` (MessageEntities): The marks the heading wears, counted over `title` — the row's own list, with no second flag saying «marked / not marked» and no mark the composition adds on its own. On the way out of the page the form substitutes the marks of the heading block here wherever this list is empty, and `widenedToHeading()` then grows a whole-field mark over the number a numbered part raised into its heading line
  - `buttons` (LinkButtons): The keyboard of `text` — the list of link buttons Telegram draws under the message, empty when the record goes out without one
  - `publishedAt` (string|null): Publication timestamp in UTC (`YYYY-MM-DD HH:MM:SS`), null for a draft
  - `createdAt` (string): Creation timestamp
  - `updatedAt` (string): Last update timestamp
  - `telegramId` (int|null): Telegram message ID (null until published)
  - `deletedAt` (string|null): Only for `publications_deleted` rows; null while the message still waits for removal from the channel
  - `partGroupId` (string|null) / `partOrder` (int|null): The group one part of a separately published publication belongs to, and its place there, counting from 1 — both null for a row that stands in no group
  - `partReleased` (bool): Whether the row stands in the queue; a part of a group is written `false` from the second row on, and true for every row that is not a part of one
  - `partReleasable` (bool): Whether the part above it has reached the channel, so this one can be started — read out of the same query (`partColumns()`), never stored
  - `messageText(): string` / `messageEntities(): MessageEntities` - The message the channel is given, composed out of `title` and `text`: the heading line — with the «Часть N» the text opens with moved to its end — over the rest of the text, and that text's entities moved behind it, while the heading carries exactly the list of `titleFormatting`. Since 2026-10-25 the composition paints nothing over that line by itself: a heading nobody marked goes out plain, and it is the form that lays the marks of the heading block over `titleFormatting` before the record is written. `headingAndBody(): array` is the same cut for a list that shows the record instead of sending it

- **`MessageEntities` (DTO, `app/shared/Telegram/Dto`)** - The inline formatting of one message text as the list the Bot API takes it in:
  - `TYPES` - The six formats carried: `bold`, `italic`, `underline`, `strikethrough`, `code`, `text_link`
  - `fromArray(array $raw): self` / `fromStored(mixed $raw): self` - Read a list, dropping entries of a type Telegram does not render and accepting the jsonb column value in every shape it arrives in (a JSON string, an already decoded list, null on a row written before the column)
  - `slice(int $start, ?int $length = null): self` - The entities of a substring re-based on its beginning, spans clipped to it and spans outside it dropped — this is what cuts a caption out of a text and a continuation out of its tail
  - `underHeading(string $heading, string $gap = "\n\n", int $moved = 0, self $own = new self()): self` - The list as it stands under a heading of its own: every span of the list moves behind the heading and the gap after it — less `$moved`, the length of the number line the heading took out of the head of the text, since the list was counted over a text that still began with it — while the heading itself wears `$own`, the marks it was saved with, and nothing else. The `empty($own) → one bold span over the whole line` branch this method answered until 2026-10-25 is gone, and an unmarked heading now moves the body and goes out plain
  - `widenedToHeading(string $title, string $heading): self` - The same list measured over the line a heading goes out as instead of over the words of its field: where the line is longer than the trimmed field — a numbered part hands its number into the heading — every span standing from zero to the end of the field is stood to the end of the line, so the mark of a whole heading covers the number too. A span the reader stopped short of the end keeps the piece it always claimed, a list whose heading gained nothing comes back as saved, and this belongs to the way out only: `assertFitsText()` measures a stored list against the words of the field, and a mark over a number the field does not hold is a record the server refuses
  - `assertFitsText(string $text): void` - Refuses a list that does not describe real spans of the text: an empty span, a span reaching past the end, an entry out of the offset order, a link with no http(s) address
  - `utf16Length(string $text): int` / `toArray()` / `toJson()` / `isEmpty()` - Counting in the units Telegram counts in, and the shapes the list takes on its way through the column, the form and the API

- **`LinkButton` (DTO, `app/shared/Telegram/Dto`)** - One link button of a message — the label and the address of one of the buttons Telegram draws under the text, the photo or the album:
  - `TEXT_MAX_LENGTH` (64) / `URL_MAX_LENGTH` (2048) - What Telegram accepts of a label and of an address; the label is counted in **bytes**, so 33 Cyrillic letters are already too long
  - `fromArray(array $raw): self` - Read a pair, trimming both halves
  - `fromStored(mixed $raw): self` - Read one stored pair — a JSON object string, an already decoded pair, the empty object, null on a row written before the column; the jsonb column as a whole is read through `LinkButtons::fromStored()`
  - `assertValid(): void` - Refuses a half-filled pair — a label with no address, an address with no label — a label over the byte limit, an address over the character limit, and an address that is not `http://`, `https://` or `tg://`
  - `isEmpty()` / `toArray()` / `toJson()` - Whether the pair is nothing at all, and the shapes it takes on its way through the column, the form and the API

- **`LinkButtons` (DTO, `app/shared/Telegram/Dto`)** - The keyboard of one message as the list the Bot API takes it in — packed into `MAX_ROWS` rows at most, the list shared between them evenly and widening downwards:
  - `MAX_BUTTONS` (10) - How many buttons one message may carry: a bound the portal gives a publication, not one the API imposes — past this point the buttons stop being an accent and become a menu
  - `MAX_ROWS` (2) - How many rows the keyboard is given: a client spreads the buttons of a row over the whole width of the message, so it is the rows, not the buttons, that turn an accent under the text into a block of their own. The preview of the form packs the list by the same bound
  - `fromPairs(array $texts, array $urls): self` - Pair the two lists of one part of the form by position, trimming each half and dropping the rows that hold nothing in either field; a half-filled row stays, so that `assertValid()` is the one that refuses it
  - `fromArray(array $raw): self` / `fromStored(mixed $raw): self` - Read the column value in every shape it arrives in: the JSON list a new row holds, the single JSON object a row written before the list holds, the empty object, `[]`, broken JSON, null on a row written before the column
  - `assertValid(): void` - Refuses more buttons than the message is given, then hands every button to `LinkButton::assertValid()`; an empty list needs nothing and passes, because no keyboard is a valid answer
  - `empty(): self` / `all()` / `count()` / `isEmpty()` / `toArray()` / `toJson()` - The list and the shapes it takes on its way through the column, the form and the API
  - `rows(): array` - The list cut into `MAX_ROWS` rows at most and shared between them evenly, the row the odd button falls to being the last one: two buttons stand one under another, three put a lone button over a pair, ten fill two rows of five. The shape follows a working bot's keyboards — in an export of 3327 of its messages with a keyboard (kept outside the repository) all 1383 keyboards of exactly two buttons are columns, and no keyboard is two buttons in a single row. What the adapter turns into the `inline_keyboard` of the message and what the preview of the form lays out under every part; an empty list gives no rows at all

- **`PublicationRepositoryInterface`** - Abstract repository used by the service to persist publications (implemented by `PublicationRepository`, which keeps all the SQL):
  - `createDraft(string $text, array $imageUrls, string $now, MessageEntities $formatting, LinkButtons $buttons, string $title): int` - Creates a draft with the highlighting of its text, its keyboard of link buttons and its heading, returns its id
  - `createPost(string $text, array $imageUrls, string $publishedAt, string $now, MessageEntities $formatting, LinkButtons $buttons, string $title): int` - Creates a scheduled post with its buttons and its heading, returns its id
  - `createPosts(array $parts, string $now): int` - Inserts the parts of one long publication as separate posts **in a single transaction** (all rows or none); `$parts` is a list of `['text' => …, 'imageUrls' => …, 'formatting' => …, 'buttons' => …, 'title' => …]` rows, so every part carries its own album, its own highlighting, its own buttons and its own heading. Returns the id of the first part
  - `createDrafts(array $parts, string $now): int` - Same, for drafts
  - `groupExists(string $groupId): bool` - Whether a group of parts with this name is already stored — the check a resubmitted form runs into before anything is written
  - `createStagedGroup(array $parts, string $groupId, string $now): int` - Inserts the parts of one publication as an ordered group **in a single transaction**, naming them `$groupId` and writing `part_released` true for the first row and false for every row below it. Returns the id of the first part
  - `findPartGroup(string $groupId): PublicationData[]` - The parts of one group that stand in the posts table, in the order of `part_order`, each with the `part_releasable` the queue reads
  - `findGroupRows(string $groupId): PublicationData[]` - Every live part of one publication no matter which block it stands in: a `UNION ALL` over the posts, drafts and deleted tables, each row carrying its `habitat` (`channel`, `post`, `draft` or `deleted`), the released flag it holds there and the number of parts its own table counts for that group
  - `releasePart(string $groupId, int $partOrder, string $publishedAt, string $now): void` - Lets one waiting part into the queue with the time it was given. The update is conditional on `part_released = FALSE`, so a second release of the same part raises `InvalidArgumentException` rather than sending a part twice
  - `updatePost(int $id, string $text, array $imageUrls, string $publishedAt, string $now, MessageEntities $formatting, LinkButtons $buttons, string $title, ?int $partOrder = null): void` - Updates a post, `created_at` untouched. `$partOrder` is the place the form gave the part: the row keeps its group and takes the rank its field stands at, which is what the two arrows of a part write back. A row of a group is refused by nothing here — the queue, and only the queue, decides when a part may go out
  - `updateDraft(int $id, string $text, array $imageUrls, string $now, MessageEntities $formatting, LinkButtons $buttons, string $title, ?int $partOrder = null): void` - Updates a draft, `created_at` untouched, and the place of the row in its group when the form gives one
  - `deletePost(int $id): PublicationData` / `deleteDraft(int $id): PublicationData` / `deleteDeleted(int $id): PublicationData` - Removes a row and returns it as it was before deletion. Each of them takes the one row it is named for, group or no group: a part that is dropped or moved to drafts leaves its publication on its own, and the rest of the group stays where it stood
  - `findPost(int $id): PublicationData|null` - Finds a post by ID
  - `findDraft(int $id): PublicationData|null` - Finds a draft by ID
  - `findDeleted(int $id): PublicationData|null` - Finds a deleted record by ID
  - `allPosts(int $limit = 0, int $offset = 0, bool $oldestFirst = false): PublicationData[]` - Gets posts (scheduled and published), newest `published_at` first; `$limit` takes that many rows starting at `$offset`, `0` means the whole list, `$oldestFirst` reverses the order
  - `allDrafts(int $limit = 0, int $offset = 0, bool $oldestFirst = false): PublicationData[]` - Gets drafts by `updated_at`, paged and ordered the same way
  - `allDeleted(int $limit = 0, int $offset = 0, bool $oldestFirst = false): PublicationData[]` - Gets soft-deleted records by `updated_at`, paged and ordered the same way
  - `countPosts(): int` / `countDrafts(): int` / `countDeleted(): int` - How many rows each list holds without paging, so a paged reader knows when it has reached the end
  - `findDueForPublishing(string $now): PublicationData[]` - Posts with an empty `telegram_id`, `part_released = TRUE` and `published_at <= now`, `id` ascending — a part that has not been released is not in the queue at all
  - `storeTelegramId(int $id, int $telegramId, string $publishedAt, string $now): void` - Stores the channel message id and corrects `published_at` to the send time
  - `insertPostWithHistory(PublicationData $post, string $now): int` / `insertDraftWithHistory(PublicationData $draft, string $now): int` / `insertDeletedWithHistory(PublicationData $record, string $now): int` - Insert a moved row preserving its original `created_at` (and `published_at` for archived records)
  - `insertChannelDeletionTicket(PublicationData $record, string $now): int` - The debt a record leaves in `publications_deleted` when it moves to drafts while the channel still holds its message: the row carries the `telegram_id`, the text and the album of the record, `deleted_at` stays empty for `deleteDue()` to stamp, and the three part columns stay empty on purpose — `findGroupRows()` would read a second row claiming the same `part_order` as one more part of the publication
  - `archiveEdited(PublicationData $post, string $now): void` - Copies a published post into `publications_edited`, carrying the group id and the place of a part when the row has them
  - `findPendingChannelDeletion(): PublicationData[]` - Soft-deleted records still awaiting removal from the channel
  - `storeDeletedAt(int $id, string $deletedAt): void` - Stamps the time a record left the channel
  - `findPendingChannelEdits(): PublicationData[]` - Archived edits still awaiting the channel update
  - `storeEditedAt(int $id, string $editedAt): void` - Stamps the time a message was replaced in the channel

- **`ForumPublicationRef`** - Reference to the forum entity a publication was created from:
  - `type` (string): Either `'topic'` or `'post'`, `isTopic()` / `isPost()` test it
  - `id` (int): ID of the forum topic or post

- **`PublicationForumLinkStoreInterface`** - Temporary store that remembers the relation between a newly created draft/post and a forum entity until the publication is finally published:
  - `remember(int $publicationId, ForumPublicationRef $ref): void` - Stores the link
  - `find(int $publicationId): ForumPublicationRef|null` - Recalls the link
  - `move(int $fromPublicationId, int $toPublicationId): void` - Moves link between publication IDs
  - `forget(int $publicationId): void` - Removes the link

- **`ForumPublicationMapGatewayInterface`** - Permanent bridge tables storing the link between a forum entity and a Telegram message (implemented by `ForumRepository`):
  - `storeTopicMapTelegramId(int $topicId, ?int $telegramId): void` - Creates the `publications_topic_map` row with an empty `telegram_id`, or stamps an existing row with the channel message id
  - `storePostMapTelegramId(int $postId, ?int $telegramId): void` - Same for `publications_post_map`
  - Both mirror the state onto the twin entity when a topic has exactly one post — see the note under the link tables above

The concrete implementations live in the **Infrastructure** sub-namespace of each module and are wired via Yii DI container.

## Service API – `PublicationsService`

The service lives in `app/shared/Publications/Service/PublicationsService.php` and implements the following public use-cases:

| Method | Description |
|--------|-------------|
| `posts(int $limit = 0, int $offset = 0, bool $oldestFirst = false): array` | Returns *published* posts (including scheduled ones that are already due), newest `published_at` first. `$limit`/`$offset` read the list a page at a time, `0` returns all of it, `$oldestFirst` flips the order. |
| `drafts(int $limit = 0, int $offset = 0, bool $oldestFirst = false): array` | Returns drafts by `updated_at`, paged and ordered the same way. |
| `deleted(int $limit = 0, int $offset = 0, bool $oldestFirst = false): array` | Returns soft-deleted records by `updated_at`, paged and ordered the same way. |
| `listTotals(): array` | `{posts, drafts, deleted}` — how many rows each of the three lists holds, so the browser knows how much further a block can scroll. |
| `saveDraft(string $text, array $imageUrls, MessageEntities $formatting, LinkButtons $buttons, string $title, ?ForumPublicationRef $forumRef = null): void` | Validates that the record carries either text or at least one of its `imageUrls`, that the message it composes out of its heading and its text is no longer than 4096 chars, that the formatting describes spans of that text and that every button of its keyboard is either whole or absent, then stores the record as a **draft**. If a forum reference is supplied, a temporary link is stored. |
| `schedulePost(string $text, array $imageUrls, MessageEntities $formatting, LinkButtons $buttons, string $title, string $publishedAt, ?ForumPublicationRef $forumRef = null, ?string $userTimezone = null): void` | Validates the composed message and its buttons, normalises the supplied date (several common formats are supported), creates a **scheduled** post (`published_at` set) and stores an optional forum link. |
| `saveParts(array $texts, array $imageGroups, array $formats, array $buttons, array $titles, string $publishedAt, string $action, ?ForumPublicationRef $forumRef = null, ?string $userTimezone = null, bool $individualParts = false, array $partTimes = [], string $partGroupId = ''): void` | Entry point of the publication form for a **new** record, which sends one text field, one album field, one formatting field, one box of button rows and one heading field per part of a long publication. Every part goes through the text validation before anything is written — and a part passes it on its own album alone, with an empty text field. A single field is saved exactly as `saveDraft()`/`schedulePost()` would. Several fields become one record each through a single repository call (`createPosts()`/`createDrafts()`), so a failure halfway leaves none of the parts behind; `$action` (`'draft'` or `'publish'`) picks the table, the forum link belongs to the first record, and its `published_at` is the form date with each following part shifted by the configured parts gap (`partsOffsetMinutes`, read through `PublicationSettingsService::intValue()` and floored by the `publish-due` cron interval), so `publishDue()` drains the parts in order. `$imageGroups` is read positionally against `$texts` — the album of part *n* is the list the form held in field *n* — and a part whose field stands empty is saved without images. `$formats` is read the same way, and the `trim()` of a part takes its formatting through the same cut (`trimFormatting()`), so a span never outlives the text it was counted over. `$buttons` is read the same way once more — the rows of that part, the shared «Кнопки-ссылки» box standing for the first one — and a part whose box stayed switched off arrives with an empty keyboard and is saved without buttons. `$titles` is read the same way a fourth time — the heading field of that part, the shared «Заголовок» field standing for the first one — and a part whose field stands empty (or holds nothing but spaces) is saved without a heading and goes out as the text it was typed with. `$individualParts` is the switch «Работать с частями по отдельности», and it changes two things: the time of a part is then `$partTimes[n]` when that field was filled, the shifted form date otherwise, and each part must be strictly later than the part above it («Время каждой следующей части должно быть позже предыдущей.»); and the rows are written by `createStagedGroup()` under `$partGroupId` — the group the form carried in its hidden field, or a fresh 32 hex characters when that field was empty or malformed — so only the first part enters the queue and the rest wait. A group id the database already holds is refused («Эта группа частей уже сохранена. Обновите форму и отправьте ещё раз.») rather than written a second time, and the draft branch ignores all three parameters: parts of a draft are ordinary rows. |
| `saveFromForm(string $text, array $imageUrls, MessageEntities $formatting, LinkButtons $buttons, string $title, string $publishedAt, string $source, ?int $sourceId, string $action, ?string $userTimezone = null): void` | The eight scenarios of the form when it holds a record opened for editing (`$source` is `post`, `draft` or `deleted`, `$action` is `'draft'` or `'publish'`): update in place, or move between the posts, drafts, edited and deleted tables. The scenario «published post + Сохранить» moves the text the form brought into the drafts table — the content of the fields, not the old content of the row — and writes the deletion ticket the channel is owed for the message it already holds. `created_at` is always preserved, `updated_at` is the moment of the change, nothing is sent to Telegram from the form. A form that holds several parts of one stored group goes to `saveGroupFromForm()` instead; the third button of the form (`'home'`) belongs to that path alone and is refused over a single record. See «Сценарии сохранения» in [README.md](README.md). |
| `formRowsOfGroup(string $groupId, string $source, int $sourceId): array` | The parts the form opens when one row of a group is clicked: every live row of the group in its place, read through `findGroupRows()`, with the rows that rest in the deleted block left there — except the one row the reader himself opened from that block, which comes with them (`$source`/`$sourceId` are the block and the id the click carried). |
| `saveGroupFromForm(array $texts, array $imageGroups, array $formats, array $buttons, array $titles, array $slots, array $partTimes, string $publishedAt, string $action, string $groupId, ?string $userTimezone = null): void` | The form of a group that is already stored: one slot per part (`publicationPartSlot[]`, the JSON of the row the field was taken out of — its id, the block the action moves it back to and its habitat), validated part by part before anything is written. `$action` is one of three, and each states its own rule about blocks. «Опубликовать» gathers the whole group into the posts table: a planned row takes the time of its own queue row, a drafted or a restored row moves into the table with it, and a row the channel already holds is edited there — the text the form brought is archived in `publications_edited` under the old `telegram_id` and the posts row is rewritten with the same text, so the publications block keeps calling it published. «Сохранить» carries the whole group into the drafts table: a drafted row keeps its row and changes only its content, a row standing in the posts table leaves that table for a new draft, and a row the channel holds writes a `publications_deleted` ticket beside it, while a row resting in the deleted block comes back as a draft and the deletion it waited for is cancelled. «Сохранить по местам» (`'home'`) rewrites every row where its slot names it, leaving that row's block, `telegram_id` and `published_at` alone, and asks the channel to edit a row that has already gone out. Only «Опубликовать» reads a queue row's time; the two saving buttons leave it to the row. A part with no slot goes to the block the action names (posts for `'publish'`, drafts for the other two), a part the form dropped goes to `publications_deleted`, and a slot whose row has already moved is refused («Часть публикации уже перемещена. Обновите форму и отправьте ещё раз.»). Every part carried into the posts table takes the queue flag of the parts above it, not its own. `created_at` is always preserved, `updated_at` is the moment of the change, nothing is sent to Telegram from the form. |
| `savePartAsDraft(string $text, array $imageUrls, MessageEntities $formatting, LinkButtons $buttons, string $title, string $groupId): int` | The «Сохранить» handle of one part of the form, pressed while that part holds no row in any block: the same checks `saveDraft()` makes (`assertPartValid()`, `MessageEntities::assertFitsText()`, `LinkButtons::assertValid()`) and the same row written through `createDraft()`, but under the group the form carries. Its `part_order` is `0` when that group is not named, and `max(part_order) + 1` over every row of the group otherwise — `nextGroupPlace()` reads `findGroupRows()`, so a part resting in the drafts or the deleted block keeps its place in the count and the new part stands behind the whole of them. Returns the id of the row it wrote, which is what the browser writes back into the slot of that part. |
| `publishDue(): array` | Drains the publishing queue: every post with an empty `telegram_id` and `part_released` true whose `published_at` has come, read by `findDueForPublishing()` in ascending `id` order. A record that reaches the channel gets the message id Telegram returned into `telegram_id`, and its `published_at` moves to the actual send time (`storeTelegramId()`); the forum map row of a record that came from a topic or a post is stamped with the same id. A record the channel refuses is asked again while the attempt budget of this one run lasts — three attempts by default (`$publishAttempts`), the wait in front of a retry growing with the attempt (`$publishRetryMicroseconds × attempt`) or taken from the `retry_after` the API asked for, whichever is longer and neither past `RETRY_WAIT_MAX_MICROSECONDS`, so a record cannot push the pass beyond the interval the task is scheduled on. A record the budget ran out on is logged once with its attempt count, skipped and left with an empty `telegram_id`, which is what makes the next run of the task take it up again; the records behind it are sent by the same pass. There is no terminal state: nothing gives a record up for good. Returns a stats array `{processed, published, failed}`, counted per record and not per attempt. |
| `deleteDue(): array` | Processes rows in `publications_deleted` with an empty `deleted_at`, removes the message from Telegram by `telegram_id` (if present), then sets `deleted_at` to current time. Returns a stats array `{processed, deleted, failed}`. Records without `telegram_id` are marked as deleted immediately (no message existed in Telegram). A delete that Telegram answers with `message to delete not found` counts as done and is stamped too: the channel already does not hold the message, so a retry could only repeat the same answer for ever. Any other failure keeps `deleted_at` empty and is retried on the next run. |
| `editDue(): array` | Processes rows in `publications_edited` with an empty `edited_at`, updates the Telegram message text by `telegram_id`, then sets `edited_at` to current time. Returns a stats array `{processed, edited, failed}`. |
| `publishDraft(int $id): void` | The «Опубликовать» button on a draft: the record moves to the posts table with `published_at` and `updated_at` set to the current time, so the periodic `publishDue()` task sends it to Telegram at once. `created_at` is preserved. |
| `scheduleDraft(int $id, string $publishedAt, ?string $userTimezone = null): void` | The «Запланировать публикацию» modal: the draft moves to the posts table with the publication time from the modal. `created_at` is preserved, `updated_at` is the current time. |
| `publishPostNow(int $id): void` | The «Опубликовать» button on a scheduled post: the post stops being scheduled — `published_at` and `updated_at` become the current time, `created_at` is untouched. A row of a group is asked for its neighbours first: `assertPartInOrder()` refuses it while the group holds an earlier part the channel does not have yet («Сначала нужно опубликовать предыдущую часть.»), so the button of a list cannot step over the sequence. |
| `releasePart(string $groupId, int $order, string $publishedAt, ?string $userTimezone = null): void` | The «Опубликовать» button of a waiting part, pressed in the list of publications: reads the group, refuses a part that is not there, one that is already in the queue and one whose part above has not reached the channel, normalises the time the button was given and refuses one that is not later than the part above, then writes the release. Only the part above holds it back — the parts below are re-timed when they are released, so a planned time of a later row is never a ceiling on this one. |
| `movePostToDraft(int $id): int` | The «Переместить в черновик» button: the post row is deleted from the posts table and inserted into the drafts table; `created_at` is preserved, `updated_at` is the moment of the move. A post that had already reached the channel leaves a debt there: a `publications_deleted` row carrying its `telegram_id` and an empty `deleted_at` is written, because the draft table holds no `telegram_id` the periodic task could delete the channel message by. A planned post owes the channel nothing and writes no such row. Returns the id of the draft row the post became — the same receipt is given back to the form of a part, whose next handle would otherwise ask for the row that is already gone. |
| `publishDeleted(int $id): void` | Restores a soft-deleted publication to the posts table with current timestamp as `published_at`, clears `telegram_id` (new message will be sent), and removes the deleted record. Used for republishing deleted content. |
| `scheduleDeleted(int $id, string $publishedAt, ?string $userTimezone = null): void` | Restores a soft-deleted publication to the posts table with the specified `publishedAt`, clears `telegram_id`, and removes the deleted record. Used for rescheduling deleted content. |
| `moveDeletedToDraft(int $id): int` | Moves a soft-deleted publication to the drafts table, clearing `telegram_id` and setting `updated_at` to current time. Used for recovering deleted content as a draft. Returns the id of the draft row the record became, for the same reason `movePostToDraft()` does. |
| `deleteDraft(int $id): int` | Moves a draft into `publications_deleted` so the pending removal is dropped and the record stays restorable. Returns the id of the deleted row the draft became. |
| `deletePost(int $id): int` | Soft-deletes a published/scheduled post by moving it to `publications_deleted` table, preserving `created_at` and `published_at`, setting `updated_at` to current time, and clearing `telegram_id` (the periodic task will handle Telegram deletion). ### Validation Rules
- A part needs either text or its own album: `assertPartValid()` refuses a part whose text is empty and whose picture list is empty with «Публикации нужны текст или хотя бы одно изображение.», while a part that brings pictures may keep its editor empty and goes out as an album with no caption (see «Альбом вместо текста» in [README.md](README.md)). A heading and a keyboard do not rescue such a part. Length is the other, separate rule: the message must not exceed 4096 characters. The limit is per record, and a long publication is split into parts by the form before saving, so each stored part stays inside it (see `saveParts()` and «Разбивка длинного текста на части» in [README.md](README.md))
- The limit falls on the message the channel is given, not on the text column alone: `assertPartValid()` measures `PublicationData::messageText()`, so a heading counts against the 4096 of its part, and a part with one is refused with «Заголовок с текстом должен быть не длиннее 4096 символов». A heading of nothing but spaces is no heading and the part is measured as its text alone
- A formatting list must describe real spans of its text (`MessageEntities::assertFitsText()`, reached from `assertPartValid()` on every write path): no empty span, nothing past the end, offsets in non-decreasing order, an http(s) address behind every `text_link`. An entry of a type Telegram does not render is dropped on read, not refused
- Every link button of a record is either a full pair or nothing (`LinkButtons::assertValid()`, called on every write path beside `assertPartValid()`): a label with no address or an address with no label, a label over 64 bytes, an address over 2048 characters and an address outside `http://`, `https://` or `tg://` all raise `InvalidArgumentException` — a flash, and no record written. The same check refuses a keyboard of more than `LinkButtons::MAX_BUTTONS` (**10**) buttons — a bound of the portal, not of the API, so the form never offers an eleventh row
- Image URLs array can be empty but must contain valid URLs when provided
- Dates must be parseable by multiple common formats (see `normalizeDate()` method)
- The parts of one group follow each other in time, and nothing else about a group is a judgement about time: `saveParts()` refuses a part whose `published_at` is not later than the part above (the form fields when the switch is thrown, the shifted slots otherwise), and `releasePart()` refuses the same answer when a waiting part is started («Время части должно быть позже времени предыдущей части.»). A part below is *not* asked to keep its planned time as a ceiling — it is re-timed at its own release, and a ceiling would let a far-future part freeze the ones above it. A group that is already stored is not measured again on the server: `saveGroupFromForm()` takes each queue row as the form gives it, and the order is asked in the field of a part instead, only for the action that publishes one (`partTimesEnforced`, false for both «Сохранить» and «Сохранить по местам»)
- The sequence holds outside the queue too: `publishPostNow()` refuses a part whose group still holds an unpublished part above it, `releasePart()` refuses a part whose neighbour above has no `telegram_id`, and `releasePart()` of the repository is a conditional update, so two clicks of the same part cannot both put it into the queue. Nothing else about a group is a refusal: `deletePost()`, `deleteDraft()`, `updatePost()` and `updateDraft()` take any row they are named for, group or no group, and the two refusals that once answered a part of a group («Часть отдельной публикации нельзя редактировать/удалить отдельно.») left the code with that reading
- Forum references are optional but when provided must contain valid source ('topic' or 'post') and positive sourceId
- The handle «Сохранить» of a part of the form names the part it means by its place, and a place the request does not hold is refused, not guessed: `publicationPartIndex` is asked against the list of texts that came with it — anything `< 0` or `>= count($texts)` answers «Часть публикации не найдена в форме.» — and the group id, which travels with the whole form, answers «Группа частей не названа.» unless it is empty or thirty-two hex characters. The album of that one part is the only one the request stores: the files are looked up under the same index (`withUploadedImages([$index => $albums[$index] ?? []])`), so pictures the reader picked for a neighbour belong to no row this action writes

### Error Handling
- `InvalidArgumentException` is thrown for validation failures (text length, date format, missing IDs, etc.)
- `TelegramApiException` is thrown when Telegram API calls fail (network issues, invalid bot token, rate limiting, etc.). It carries the HTTP `errorCode` and the `retryAfter` of a flood wait, and answers `isMessageGone()` for the one failure that is really a success: a delete of a message the channel no longer has. `isMessageUnchanged()` is its sibling for the other such answer, `message is not modified`: an edit that asks a message for what that message already is.
- `RuntimeException` is thrown when Telegram bot is not configured (missing TELEGRAM_BOT_TOKEN)

## Telegram Integration – `ChannelService`

Located at `app/shared/Telegram/Service/ChannelService.php`. The service is a thin wrapper around the low-level `TelegramChannelClientInterface` and adds business-level validation.

| Method | Purpose |
|--------|---------|
| `isConfigured(): bool` | Returns **true** if a bot client instance is provided. |
| `channelInfo(): ChannelInfo` | Retrieves basic channel information (id, title, username, description). |
| `updateDescription(string $description): void` | Validates that the description does not exceed **255** characters and updates the channel description via the Telegram API. The new description is archived in the `publishedDescriptions` repository. |
| `publishedDescriptions(): array` | Returns archived channel descriptions (newest first). |
| `publishText(string $text, MessageEntities $entities = new MessageEntities(), LinkButtons $buttons = new LinkButtons()): int` | Sends a plain-text message (1–4096 chars) with its entities and, when the record has any, its link buttons as the keyboard of the message. Returns the Telegram `message_id`. |
| `publishPhotos(string $text, array $photoUrls, MessageEntities $entities = new MessageEntities(), LinkButtons $buttons = new LinkButtons()): int` | Sends one or many photos (up to 10 per API call). The first photo receives a caption (max 1024 chars), cut at the last line break that still fits so a line is never split. An empty text is not a refusal any more: the photos then go out with no caption at all, which is the album Telegram makes of pictures alone, and there is nothing left to send as a continuation. What does not fit into the caption is sent once as a continuation text message after the photos, never as a repeat of the whole text. The formatting follows the same cut: `MessageEntities::slice()` keeps the spans of the caption in the caption and re-bases the rest onto the continuation. Only the album that carries the caption is given the keyboard, so a text cut into several albums leaves its buttons on the first of them. Returns the `message_id` of the first photo. |
| `pinPost(int $messageId): void` | Pins the given message in the channel. |
| `deletePost(int $messageId): void` | Deletes a message from the channel. |
| `editPostText(int $messageId, string $text, MessageEntities $entities = new MessageEntities(), LinkButtons $buttons = new LinkButtons()): void` | Edits the text of an existing message (same validation as `publishText`) — entities, keyboard and all. |

Entities are the only way formatting reaches the channel: `parse_mode` is never sent, the text goes out as plain characters, and the spans go with it as `entities` of a text message or `caption_entities` of a photo — an empty list sends neither (`NutgramChannelClient::toMessageEntities()`). The other side is sent the same way: `sendPhotoMessage()` and `sendPhotoGroupMessage()` leave out both `caption` and `caption_entities` when the caption is empty, because an empty string would be a caption of zero length rather than its absence.

A link button reaches the channel as `reply_markup`: `NutgramChannelClient::toKeyboard()` builds an `InlineKeyboardMarkup` with one `addRow()` per `LinkButtons::rows()` entry — `MAX_ROWS` rows at most, the list shared between them evenly, the wide row last — out of `InlineKeyboardButton(text, url)` objects, and an empty keyboard builds nothing at all — a publication without buttons sends the very same request it sent before. `sendMediaGroup` takes no `reply_markup`, so the keyboard of a part with an album is attached to its first photo by a following `editMessageReplyMarkup`, and only when there are buttons to attach. The first photo of an album the queue has already sent can be standing in the channel wearing that very keyboard while its record is still queued, and the edit of such a message answers 400 `message is not modified` — the refusal of a change that needed no change. `sendPhotoGroupMessage()` reads that answer as the send it describes: the album is there, its button is on it, and the message id goes back to the queue, which stamps the record instead of asking the channel for the same album on the next run. Any other refusal of the edit is the failure it has always been. `editMessageText` keeps the keyboard that hung on the message when the parameter is omitted, which is why every edit answers with one: the buttons' markup, or an empty `InlineKeyboardMarkup` that drops it (`toReplacementKeyboard()`).

The channel never sees the columns as they stand: `publishToTelegram()` and `editInTelegram()` send `PublicationData::messageText()` with `messageEntities()`, so what goes out is the record's text with its heading composed in front of it — the heading line bold, the «Часть N» the text opened with moved to the end of that line — or left where the heading already wore it, since `headingCarriesItsNumber()` recognises a heading the form numbered in front, and since the form no longer writes that line into a part whose heading carries the number, the message is numbered by exactly one of the two fields — and the spans of the text behind it. A record without a heading composes into its own text byte for byte, which is every record written before the column existed. See «Заголовок части» in [README.md](README.md).

An album goes out as files rather than as links: `publishToTelegram()` hands the pictures of a record to `prepareImageUrlsForTelegram()`, which fetches every one of them through the injected `ForumHttpClientInterface` — the same client the parsers read the forum with, and the only HTTP client the queue is wired with — writes the answer into `publication_img_*` in the temp directory, and gives the channel that path. Which host a picture stands on decides nothing: `forum.awd.ru` answers only a client holding its session, while hosts like `foto.awd.ru` serve the portal a JPEG and answer the channel's own fetcher with `wrong type of the web page content`, and bytes that already arrived here cannot be refused a second time over there. A picture this side could not fetch keeps its address in the album, so the channel gets its own attempt at it and the record is not refused over one broken file; `NutgramChannelClient::isLocalFile()` tells the two cases apart by `file_exists()` — a path is uploaded as `InputFile`, an address is passed to the API as media. The name a download takes comes from the bytes it answered with, unless its address ends in one of the picture extensions listed in `PublicationsService::IMAGE_EXTENSIONS`.

A download is not removed on the spot: the file has done its work once the message carrying it is sent, and nothing comes back for it later — an edit replaces the text of a message, and a re-send reads the album again from its addresses. `TempImageCleanup` (`app/shared/Publications/Infrastructure/TempImageCleanup.php`, run by `telegram/clean-temp` once a day by default) takes the files older than `MAX_AGE_SECONDS` (an hour): a younger one can still belong to an album being sent by a queue pass running at the same moment, since the client reads the file while the request goes out. The sweep looks at that one name and at nothing else — the container's temp directory also holds the crontab `entrypoint.sh` writes for supercronic and the mounted composer cache, and neither is the queue's to remove. The name itself is written in one place, `TempImageCleanup::PREFIX`, which is what `downloadImage()` builds its path from.

Because this same client also visits hosts that have nothing to do with the forum, its cookie jar is kept per host: the phpBB session is sent to `forum.awd.ru` alone, and a cookie another host handed out is never offered to the forum. See «Настройки парсера» in [PARSER.md](PARSER.md).

All methods may throw `TelegramApiException` (API failure) and `RuntimeException` when the bot token is missing.

The client waits 30 seconds for an answer (`NutgramChannelClient::REQUEST_TIMEOUT`, given to Nutgram through `Configuration::fromArray(['timeout' => …])`). The five seconds Nutgram asks for by default are not the time the API takes to answer but the time the connection to it takes — the first call of a run spends most of it negotiating the route, measured on the local portal at about fifteen seconds — and a task that never gets past that is a task that fails again every five minutes.

The limits Telegram itself imposes are public constants of the class — `TEXT_MAX_LENGTH` (4096), `CAPTION_MAX_LENGTH` (1024), `DESCRIPTION_MAX_LENGTH` (255), `ALBUM_MAX_PHOTOS` (10) and `PARTS_NUMBERING_RESERVE` (16 characters the «Часть N» heading keeps out of the text budget) — and of `LinkButton`, which holds `TEXT_MAX_LENGTH` (64 bytes of a button label) and `URL_MAX_LENGTH` (2048 characters of its address) — because the publications form reads the first two to decide where a long text has to be split, and the settings page lists all of them through `PublicationSettingsService::telegramLimits()`. None of them is editable from the portal. `LinkButtons::MAX_BUTTONS` (10 buttons under one message) belongs to the same kind of check but is not in that list: the Bot API would accept a larger keyboard, and it is the portal that stops the form from asking.

## Console Commands (`app/commands/TelegramController.php`)

The console controller exposes the above functionality to the command line and is intended to be used by cron jobs or developers.

| Command | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| `php yii telegram/index` | Shows current channel information (id, title, username, description). |
| `php yii telegram/set-description "<desc>"` | Updates the channel description. Returns an error code if validation fails or the bot is not configured. |
| `php yii telegram/test-post "<text>" [--pin=0|1]` | Sends a test text message. Optionally pins the message. |
| `php yii telegram/publish-due` | Executes `PublicationsService::publishDue()` - publishes all due scheduled posts. |
| `php yii telegram/delete-due` | Executes `PublicationsService::deleteDue()` - removes messages from Telegram for soft-deleted publications. |
| `php yii telegram/edit-due` | Executes `PublicationsService::editDue()` - updates the text of edited publications in Telegram. |
| `php yii telegram/clean-temp` | Executes `TempImageCleanup::run()` - removes the `publication_img_*` files the queue downloaded for its albums and needs no more. |

These commands are typically scheduled by **supercronic** in the `telegram` Docker Compose service, using the environment variables defined in `.env` (see below). They can also be run manually from the `app` container.

## Cron Scheduling (environment variables)

| Variable | Default | Description |
|----------|---------|-------------|
| `TELEGRAM_PUBLISH_CRON_SCHEDULE` | `*/5 * * * *` | How often `telegram/publish-due` should run. |
| `TELEGRAM_DELETE_CRON_SCHEDULE` | `*/5 * * * *` | How often `telegram/delete-due` should run. |
| `TELEGRAM_EDIT_CRON_SCHEDULE` | `*/5 * * * *` | How often `telegram/edit-due` should run. |
| `TELEGRAM_TEMP_CLEAN_CRON_SCHEDULE` | `0 4 * * *` | How often `telegram/clean-temp` should sweep the downloaded album pictures out of the temp directory. |

These variables are read by the **docker-compose** entrypoint (or by a host scheduler) and passed to the container. Adjust them according to your desired frequency.

The four jobs run in the `telegram` container, not in the `parser` one: `docker/php-cli/entrypoint.sh` builds the crontab from `CRON_ROLE`, and only the role `telegram` writes these four lines. The container is built from the same image as the parsers, so nothing about the commands changes — where they run is a matter of which schedule lines the crontab gets. The `parser` container holds the four forum scans and has no bot token at all.

`TELEGRAM_PUBLISH_CRON_SCHEDULE` is also passed to the `app` container, because the settings page needs it: `PublicationSettingsService::cronIntervalMinutes()` turns the schedule into a number of minutes, and that number is the lower bound of the gap between the parts of one publication. The portal shows the schedule read-only and never edits it — the cron of the container is not something a page can change.

## Configuration (`.env`)

The Telegram integration requires two variables:

```
TELEGRAM_BOT_TOKEN=xxxx:xxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxx
TELEGRAM_CHANNEL_ID=@your_channel_username   # or numeric channel ID
```

If the token is missing the service will throw a `RuntimeException` and all Telegram-related commands will exit with the `CONFIG` exit code.

Three more variables left the source code and are read from the environment:

```
# Signs the session and CSRF cookies (app/config/web.php); empty key = no request
# carrying one of them is accepted. Generate: php -r "echo bin2hex(random_bytes(32));"
COOKIE_VALIDATION_KEY=
# Both are read by app/web/index.php and app/yii; without them the app is production
YII_ENV=dev
YII_DEBUG=1
```

None of the three is displayed on a settings page: a page that lists tunables is not where a secret belongs.

That orphan is what put `part_group_id`, `part_order` and `part_released` into `publications_post` in both databases, with the `(part_group_id, part_order)` index under its own name `idx_publications_post_part_order`, and what put `part_group_id` and `part_order` into the other three publications tables — read here as history rather than as the state of the repository: before `m261017_000001_keep_part_state_outside_posts` was amended on 2026-10-19 no file of the repository wrote a group column anywhere but to `publications_post`, and the columns standing in all four tables of both databases left the orphan as their only possible author. So `m261010_000001_add_publication_part_sequence`, the file this feature ships, could never run here: `yii migrate` reached it, found the first column standing and stopped with `Duplicate column`. On 2026-10-12 it was written into both histories with `yii migrate/mark m261010_000001_add_publication_part_sequence`, and `yii migrate` and `yii migrate/new` now answer «No new migrations found. Your system is up-to-date.» in `yii_dev` and in `yii_test`, with `codecept run Unit` still `OK (197 tests, 746 assertions)` after the mark. Two differences between these databases and that file stand, and both are cosmetic: the index here is named `idx_publications_post_part_order` rather than `…_part_sequence`, and `part_group_id` is `character varying(36)` where the file asks for `string(32)` — a group id is 32 hex characters, so nothing can overflow. A fresh database gets exactly what the file writes, and without it none of the three columns exists there at all: every read of `publications_post` that names them answers `column "part_group_id" does not exist`. `m261017_000001_keep_part_state_outside_posts`, the file this change ships, is by contrast a real row in both histories now — applied 2026-10-19, at 07:59:05 UTC in `yii_dev` and 07:58:50 UTC in `yii_test` — and no migration file of the repository is missing from either. The amended file is a row of both histories already, so it does not run here again: `yii_dev` and `yii_test` keep the columns the orphan laid — `part_group_id` among them as `character varying(36)` where the file asks for `string(32)` — and that difference stays on purpose. What the file now builds on its own was proved 2026-10-19 against an empty scratch database (`yii_fresh_proof`, dropped after the run): `yii migrate` applied all 23 files in order, `m261017` laid `part_group_id` and `part_order` into `publications_draft`, `publications_deleted` and `publications_edited`, `part_released` into the first two, and then its two indexes over `(part_group_id, part_order)`; `information_schema` answered `character varying(32)` for every group id, three `…_part_sequence` indexes stood, `publications_edited` held no released flag, and `codecept run Unit --filter 'PublicationRepositoryTest|PublicationsServiceReleasePartTest'` came back `OK (40 tests, 152 assertions)` over that schema. The gap named in «Domain Model» is therefore closed: a fresh database no longer stops on the index. The publications columns `formatting` and `button` are `jsonb`, and `title` is `text` in both databases.

Both databases are reached through `DB_DSN`, and `app/config/test_db.php` falls back to `yii_test` only when that variable is empty — while the `app` container exports the address of the development one. A suite therefore has to be started with the name of the database spelled out:

```
docker compose exec -T -u appuser \
  -e DB_DSN="pgsql:host=postgres;port=5432;dbname=yii_test" \
  app php vendor/bin/codecept run Unit
```

Started any other way, `PublicationRepositoryTest` — whose `_before()` truncates `publications_post`, `publications_draft` and `publications_deleted` — would empty the data being worked on.

Run that way on 2026-10-06, `Unit` is green: 180 tests, 700 assertions. It is the first time an entity list, a keyboard of several link buttons and a heading have gone through a real PostgreSQL write and read instead of a harness — what `createDrafts()` and `createPosts()` put into the three new columns of each part of a long publication, and what each list hands back with the row the value belongs to. The parts of one draft share a single `updated_at`, which is what `allDrafts()` orders by, so their relative order is the database's to choose and the assertion matches each heading against the text of its own row rather than against a position.

`Functional` is not green in the current image. A run on 2026-10-06 produced 10 tests, 4 errors and 3 failures: the four `ContactFormCest` tests error because the app image carries neither GD nor Imagick, which the captcha widget of `/contact` requires; three `LoginFormCest` checks still look for `Logout (admin)`, while `/` renders `dashboard.php`, whose user menu says «Выйти» and names nobody. The other three login scenarios passed. These failures do not exercise publications.

What the browser has been made to do is a click through `/publications`: a publication of two parts with a heading in each of the two fields, a real POST of `publicationPartTitle[]`, the draft it wrote reopened by a double-click on its row, and the composed «Заголовок. Часть N» line watched in the preview and in the counters of the form.

Still unverified is the live Telegram channel: what Telegram accepts of an overlapping entity list, of a keyboard standing under an album, and of a heading that has taken the «Часть N» line out of the text. Nothing has been sent during these checks. The `telegram` service owns `telegram/publish-due`, `telegram/delete-due`, `telegram/edit-due` and `telegram/clean-temp`; the separate `parser` service runs forum scans. The settings are also not covered by Codeception tests: clamping on read, refusal on write, the floor derived from the cron schedule, and both settings pages rest on the service implementation and manual checks.
